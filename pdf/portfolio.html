Yuzu Onsen Capybaras

The shop and the town

Yuzu Onsen Capybaras

The shop

Seven capybaras live at the shop, each with a personality of its own, and they get on with their day whether you help or not: napping, eating, paddling among the pool floats, soaking in the hot spring and sunbathing in the yard, with a little icon overhead to show how each one feels. Feed them, pat them, name them and dress them up while customers drop in, pay and leave their stars.

The town

Step out of the door and there’s a whole town. Thirteen townsfolk keep days of their own, each with tastes, a birthday, letters and stories that unfold as you become friends, and six neighbors on Pine Lantern Street have stories too. There’s a garden to grow, a forest to forage and fish in, a notice board of requests, an old bathhouse to bring back to life, and a book of wishes that changes the town as you fill it.

Under the mountain, and on your desktop

Yuzu Onsen Capybaras

Under the mountain

Under the mountain shrine, the old spring keepers’ tunnels go down thirty floors to the source of the town’s hot water. You knock open rocks for ore, crystals and fossils, find each ladder by its wisp of steam, ride the steam lifts and fish the underground pools. There are no monsters: just the warm dark and your lantern.

The desk pet

A spin-off app puts one of the shop’s capybaras on your desktop: it ambles along the bottom of the screen, and a toolbar gives it snacks, a hot bath, a paddling pool, a nap or the wardrobe. It runs on macOS and Windows.

Play it

Play the first winter free in your browser, on a computer or a phone: 28 days and all four winter festivals, with keyboard and mouse, touch or a controller, in English or Simplified Chinese. A desktop version with 20 achievements is coming to Steam.

Credits

Development led by Lily with AI tools: the code was written with Claude Code, the pixel art generated with AI image tools and the text written with the help of an AI language model. The music and sound effects are synthesized by the game as you play.

The Adventures of Edgeworth

Courtroom regulars, blocks and hearts

The Adventures of Edgeworth

The idea

Lily built the game around two things she loved: the little monster-adventure games on the 4399 web-game site she played as a child, and Capcom’s Ace Attorney. So the hero is the prosecutor Miles Edgeworth, the enemies are courtroom regulars, and the blocks are the series’ “Hold it!” and “Objection!” speech bubbles.

  • Miles Edgeworth walks, jumps, stomps.
  • Winston Payne paces; land on his head.
  • Manfred von Karma can’t be stomped.
  • Wendy Oldbag rises out of pipes.
  • Dick Gumshoe points at a hidden block.

How it works

Nine classes, about 1,400 lines, no game engine: a double-buffered 900 × 600 window, and the player, every enemy and the end flag each on its own thread. Collision works on the tile grid:

//Cannot move to the right
if(ob.getX()==this.x+60 &&
        (ob.getY()+50>this.y && ob.getY()-50<this.y)){
    if(ob.getType() != 3){
        canRight = false;
    }
}

From Edgeworth.java. Type 3 is the hidden block: you can walk through it until it’s revealed.

Every scene is a 15 × 10 grid of 60-pixel tiles, planned in a spreadsheet, one colored cell per block. Scene 3 is a face, scene 5 a heart, and scene 4’s pillars are too tall to climb until Gumshoe’s hidden “Objection!” block appears.

Game, levels, sprites and original code: Lily. 2026 browser port and Java update: built with AI coding tools. Characters from Capcom’s Ace Attorney, in a non-commercial fan game; forest animation by Anas Abdin.

AI Recipe Remix

From the fridge to the plate

AI Recipe Remix

The idea

Most recipe sites start from a dish; this one starts from your fridge. Type what you have, choose a diet and a time limit, and it suggests three realistic recipes with quantities, steps, substitutions, allergens and tips, plus what you’d still need to buy.

How it works

Recipes and photos come from two separate requests, so the recipe cards appear within seconds and each photo fades in when it’s ready. The Express server asks the model for JSON in a fixed shape and keeps the OpenAI key private.

Try it

The demo on the website plays back three sample kitchens. To cook up new recipes, run the app with your own OpenAI key.

Tic-Tac-Toe vs. AI

Rules versus search

Tic-Tac-Toe vs. AI

Her rule-based AI

Lily’s opponent plays by five rules, checked in order:

  1. Win if it can.
  2. Block you if you’re about to win.
  3. Take the center.
  4. Take a free corner.
  5. Otherwise, take a side.

It plays sensibly and can be beaten: take a corner, then the opposite corner, and your block creates a fork it can’t answer.

Three opponents

Easy mostly picks a random free square, but pounces on a win about half the time.

Medium plays by her five rules.

Hard uses minimax: it searches to the end of the game, assumes you always play your best, and picks the best guaranteed result. Alpha-beta pruning keeps each search to a few milliseconds.

Seeing the AI think

With “Show AI thinking” on, every empty square shows the minimax verdict for it: a forced win, a draw or a square that would let you win. You can watch a move turn from draw to loss the moment you set up a fork.

16,752Possible games against Hard. It loses none.
