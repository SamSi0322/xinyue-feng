Tic-Tac-Toe vs. AI

A small browser game made for a class unit on playing against AI, and a neat way to compare three kinds of opponent: one that plays loosely, one that follows a few good rules, and one that searches every possible game and never loses.

The original

The assignment was to build a game you play against an AI and put it online. Lily’s version gives the computer five rules, checked in order: win if it can, block you if you’re about to win, take the centre, take a free corner, otherwise take a side.

// 3. Take center
if (board[4] === "") {
  return 4;
}

// 4. Take a corner
const corners = [0, 2, 6, 8];
const availableCorners = corners.filter(index => board[index] === "");

From her original getBestMove(), which the game still uses as its Medium opponent.

It plays sensibly and is fun to beat, and it can be beaten. Take a corner; when it takes the centre, take the opposite corner. Its next move is always a corner, and that leaves you a fork: two lines that each need one more X. It can only block one of them.

Three opponents

  • Easy mostly picks a random free square, but grabs a winning move about half the time, so it still feels like it’s trying.
  • Medium is the original rule-based AI, kept as written.
  • Hard uses minimax. On each turn it plays out every possible continuation of the game, assumes you will always make your best move, and picks the move with the best guaranteed result. Faster wins score higher than slower ones. Alpha–beta pruning skips branches that can’t change the answer, so the search takes milliseconds. The best you can do against it is a draw.

Seeing the AI think

With Show AI thinking switched on, every empty square shows the minimax verdict for it, from the computer’s side: a forced win, a draw, or a square that would let you win. It makes the algorithm visible. You can watch a move go from “draw” to “loss” for the AI the moment you set up a fork, and see why Medium’s corner move is a mistake.

After each AI move, a short note says which of Lily’s rules it just followed, or how minimax chose, and points out when the move was a mistake.

Portfolio edition · 2026

The game was extended for this portfolio. Her rule-based AI and the original files are kept in the repository; the additions are:

  • Easy and Hard opponents, a choice of who moves first, and separate scores for each difficulty.
  • The “Show AI thinking” overlay, a note explaining each AI move, and a short explainer for each strategy.
  • A redesign that matches this site, with drawn-on X and O marks and an animated winning line.
  • Full keyboard play and screen-reader labels for every square.
  • Automated tests, including one that plays every possible game against Hard, with you moving first and with the AI moving first, and checks that it never loses.
